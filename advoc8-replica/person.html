Biography

Anthony, born on 2 March 1963, grew up in Camperdown, an inner-western Sydney suburb. He lived in social housing with his single mum, who suffered from chronic rheumatoid arthritis and lived on the pension. Anthony went to school at St Joseph’s Primary School in Camperdown and St Mary’s College in Sydney and joined the Australian Labor Party while still in his teens. His first job out of school was at a Commonwealth Bank branch, before studying a Bachelor of Economics at the University of Sydney, where he was also involved in student politics.

Anthony went on to work as a researcher for Tom Uren, a Labor MP and Minister at the time, and spent over ten years serving the Labor party as the President of Young Labor, rising to Assistant General Secretary of the NSW Labor Party before briefly working as a policy adviser to then NSW Premier, Bob Carr.

In 1996, Anthony was elected to the Federal seat of Grayndler, the electorate in which he grew up. During his early years in Parliament, Anthony held several Shadow Parliamentary Secretary and Shadow Ministerial roles, including Shadow Parliamentary Secretary for Family and Community Services from 1998 to 2001, Shadow Minister for Ageing and Seniors from 2001 to 2002, and Shadow Minister for Employment Services and Training from 2002 to 2004. He also served as Shadow Minister for Environment and Heritage from 2004 to 2006, and as Shadow Minister for Water from 2005 to 2006.

Upon Labor’s victory in 2007, he was appointed to the Cabinet, serving as the Minister for Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development and Local Government from 2007 to 2010. He continued as Minister for Infrastructure and Transport until 2013 and served as the Leader of the House throughout the Rudd and Gillard Governments. In 2013, he was appointed Deputy Prime Minister and took on the portfolios of Minister for Regional Development and Local Government and Minister for Broadband, Communications and the Digital Economy.

Following the 2013 election, Anthony returned to the Shadow Cabinet, holding portfolios including Infrastructure and Transport, Tourism, and Cities, before later taking on an expanded role covering Infrastructure, Transport, Cities and Regional Development. In May 2019, he was unanimously elected as the Leader of the Australian Labor Party following the devastating loss of an election that most polls had slated Labor to win. He served as the Leader of the Opposition until 2022. In May 2022, he took the party to victory and became Australia’s 31st Prime Minister, and was re-elected to the role in 2025.

He is currently the Prime Minister of Australia.

His key policy areas of interest are focused on building a stronger future through direct cost-of-living relief and expanded social services. Anthony is overseeing a significant tax reform agenda, delivering consecutive tax cuts for all taxpayers in 2026 and 2027 and introducing a $1,000 instant tax deduction for workers to simplify returns. He is a strong defender of universal healthcare, recently launching the 1800 Medicare advice line and expanding free Urgent Care Clinics. He is focused on intergenerational equity, having wiped 20% off student debt and made Free TAFE permanent to boost local skills. He is also focused on pursuing major housing reforms to deliver 1.2 million new homes and 5% deposits for first home buyers, while advancing gender equality through paid parental leave extensions and the inclusion of superannuation on those payments. Furthermore, he remains committed to integrity in politics through the establishment and oversight of the National Anti-Corruption Commission and is leading a "Future Made in Australia" strategy to revitalise local manufacturing and clean energy. He is a leading figure of the Labor Left faction.

Anthony currently resides in Copacabana. He also has one son. In his spare time, Anthony is a rock music fan and a hobby DJ. He is also a lifelong supporter of the South Sydney Rabbitohs NRL team.
